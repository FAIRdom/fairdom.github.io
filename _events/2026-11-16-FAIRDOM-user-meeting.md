 * 14:30 - **Timo Mühlhaus** (RPTU University, Germany)

   _"ARC it the ISA way"_

   <sup>Annotated Research Contexts (ARCs) provide a structured, FAIR-oriented framework for organizing research data, metadata, and computational workflows. A key conceptual distinction is the separation between the workspace, where research data and metadata are actively created, annotated, and maintained, and the publication space, where research outputs are represented as FAIR Digital Objects to enable FAIR data publication, discovery, and reuse. Building on the RO-Crate standard, the ARC profile collection introduces a modular approach to describing research objects while supporting interoperability, extensibility, and reuse. This approach is founded on three core profiles: Administrative Crate, which captures administrative metadata and attribution; Process Core Crate, which represents experimental and computational processes and their provenance; and Semantic Designation, which enables structured semantic annotation of research entities. Together, these profiles establish coherent annotation principles for expressing research context and meaning. A central concept is the composition of profiles through inheritance, specialization, and multityping, allowing research entities to fulfill multiple semantic roles and combine complementary metadata models without unnecessary duplication. This modular architecture provides the foundation for ISA ARCs, which integrate the Investigation–Study–Assay (ISA) model as a first-class representation within the ARC ecosystem.
Within DataPLANT, ISA ARCs are first-class citizens, placing ISA-based research data annotation at the core of research data management. Dedicated tools and services, complemented by Data Steward support, enable researchers to create, annotate, validate, and maintain ISA-structured research data throughout the research lifecycle. This combination of a shared conceptual model, practical tooling, and human expertise supports consistent metadata annotation while accommodating diverse experimental workflows and disciplinary requirements.
Together, these concepts establish a flexible, machine-actionable foundation for representing complex research contexts, connecting active research workflows with FAIR data publication, and enabling semantic clarity, provenance, and interoperability across research data infrastructures.</sup>
